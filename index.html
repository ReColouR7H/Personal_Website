About me

I am a Peruvian economist working at the intersection of public policy, economic regulation, and the Water and Sanitation sector. My work is grounded in rigorous quantitative analysis—applied econometrics, data science, and machine learning—through which I analyze large-scale datasets and national surveys to support evidence-based decision-making.

I have led the development of investment portfolios and financial planning for large-scale sectoral initiatives, as well as the design of tariff models for water and drainage services. My research focuses on rural water systems, particularly on chlorination practices and the institutional factors that shape service sustainability. I am driven by a strong commitment to public impact, aiming to contribute to the design of policies that reduce infrastructure gaps and promote equitable and sustainable development.
