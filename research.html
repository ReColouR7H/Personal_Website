Chlorination challenges: evidence from Water For People’s experience

An analysis of the conditions that sustain chlorination in rural water systems in Asunción, Cascas and Reque.

Lead author
Andrés Soto Ticse
Scope
Asunción, Cascas and Reque, Peru. Study period: 2021 to 2025.
Publication context
Water For People’s experience with rural water systems.
Document language
Spanish
Read original document ↗

Research question

Which factors are associated with adequate chlorination, and which measures could improve its continuity in the rural systems studied? The note examines community management and the operational constraints faced by service providers.

Data and methods

The study combines Water For People records with DATASS data for 2021 to 2025. The final sample contains 549 observations. It uses logistic regression weighted by household counts, with standard errors clustered by community. Interviews with operators and municipal technical staff complement the statistical analysis.

Findings

Water quality monitoring by community water boards, known as JASS, shows the most consistent association with adequate chlorination. Reported odds ratios reach 12; this compares outcome odds and does not mean that the probability increases twelvefold. Fees that cover operating costs support continued access to supplies. Interviews identify dosing difficulties and resistance to the taste and smell of chlorinated water.

Policy relevance

The recommendations prioritise community monitoring, operating finance and ongoing technical support. Chlorine provision requires follow-up to sustain its use. Findings describe associations in the districts studied; they do not provide causal estimates or nationally representative results.

Review of experiences aimed at eliminating open defecation in Latin America and the Caribbean

A regional review of sanitation interventions and the conditions needed to sustain their use in communities facing access gaps.

Lead author
Andrés Soto Ticse
Scope
Latin America and the Caribbean. Documentation from 2010 to 2025.
Publication context
Regional evidence review aligned with SDG 6.2.
Document language
Spanish
Read original document ↗

Objective

Identify practices and lessons that can support the elimination of open defecation in Latin America and the Caribbean. The report examines sanitation expansion, with attention to rural contexts and populations facing vulnerability.

Methods

The review combines academic and institutional literature with semi-structured interviews with four specialists. It identifies 17 relevant experiences from 13 documentary studies and expert contributions. The comparison considers evidence of reduced open defecation and the potential to adapt interventions to other territories.

Findings

The reviewed experiences highlight community participation and support after facilities are installed. Maintenance funding gaps and institutional discontinuity can lead to abandonment. Interviews provide insights into technologies suited to local water availability and service models for rural and peri-urban areas.

Recommendations and scope

The report recommends funding service operations, strengthening local capacity and monitoring sustained use of facilities. It also calls for gender and intercultural approaches in implementation. Evidence quality varies across experiences; the review offers comparative lessons rather than a common causal impact estimate for all programmes.
